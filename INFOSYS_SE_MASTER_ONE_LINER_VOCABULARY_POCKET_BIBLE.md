## 🚂 Travel & Mobile Revision Protocol (1 Nov to 5 Nov Exam Sprint)
1. **Do NOT panic about 10,000 dictionary words:** Infosys SE tests high-utility verbal comprehension, root morphology, and contextual precision, NOT esoteric archaic vocabulary.
2. **Focus on the 1-Liner Hindi Hook & Synonyms:** In the test, 80% of vocabulary questions are solved by recognizing whether a word has a positive (+), negative (-), or neutral connotation.

4. **Review Prepositions Daily:** Fixed preposition traps ('cope with', 'abide by', 'look forward to + V-ing') are guaranteed 2 to 3 marks in the Verbal section.

## ⚖️ Part 6: 10 Most Confusing Homophones & Diction Traps

| Confusing Pair | Distinction & Exam Application |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Complement vs Compliment** | Complement = something that completes or brings to perfection (e.g., 'The caching layer complements the database'). Compliment = polite expression of praise (e.g., 'He received a compliment on his architecture'). |
| **Principal vs Principle** | Principal = head of an organization, or primary/main (e.g., 'The principal cause of the bug'). Principle = fundamental truth, moral rule, or scientific law (e.g., 'SOLID principles of OOP'). |
| **Stationary vs Stationery** | Stationary (with 'a') = not moving, fixed in place. Stationery (with 'e' as in Envelope) = writing paper, pens, and office supplies. |
| **Affect vs Effect** | Affect (usually Verb) = to influence or produce a change in (e.g., 'Network latency affects throughput'). Effect (usually Noun) = result or consequence (e.g., 'The effect of the patch was immediate'). |
| **Eminent vs Imminent** | Eminent = famous, respected, distinguished within a profession. Imminent = about to happen very soon, impending (e.g., 'System failure is imminent'). |
| **Illicit vs Elicit** | Illicit = illegal, forbidden by law or rules. Elicit = to draw forth or evoke a response or information (e.g., 'The interviewer elicited detailed technical answers'). |
| **Council vs Counsel** | Council = an advisory, deliberative, or administrative body. Counsel = advice given, or an attorney/advisor representing someone. |
| **Allusion vs Illusion** | Allusion = an indirect or passing reference to something. Illusion = a deceptive appearance, false impression, or optical trick. |
| **Discrete vs Discreet** | Discrete (with separate 'e's) = individually separate, distinct, and unconnected. Discreet (with double 'ee' together) = careful, prudent, keeping secrets. |
| **Precede vs Proceed** | Precede = come before in time, order, or rank. Proceed = carry on or continue an action, move forward. |

# Topic 05: Infosys WAT Evaluation Rubric and Writing Guide

## Section VII — Official Exam Criteria, AI Scoring Engines & Blueprint Guide

### 1. Official Guidelines from Course Syllabus
![Official Guidelines](file:///{IMAGES_DIR.as_posix()}/infosys_essay_writing_topics_page_01.png)

1. **Word Limit Adherence:** The essay should ideally range from **200 to 400 words**. Strictly respect the minimum and maximum boundaries indicated on the active test interface (some modern shifts recommend 150–250 words; write within the specified bounds).
2. **Clarity of Expression:** Avoid overly esoteric jargon, convoluted sentence structures, and lengthy fictional stories.
3. **Grammatical Perfection:** Ensure zero grammatical errors, subject-verb agreement slips, punctuation lapses, or spelling mistakes.
4. **Structured Multi-Paragraph Layout:** Divide the essay into distinct, cohesive paragraphs to deliver an organized visual and logical impression.
5. **Tripartite Architecture:**
   - **Introduction/Definition:** Define the topic scope, introduce central themes, and state a clear thesis.
   - **Body Paragraphs:** Present balanced arguments, empirical illustrations, and thoughtful nuances.
   - **Conclusion:** Deliver a crisp, decisive synthesis emphasizing forward-looking solutions.

### 2. Automated AI Grading Rubric Criteria

| Evaluation Criterion | Weightage | What the AI Evaluator Probes |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Syntactic Coherence** | 30% | Grammatically sound clauses, correct tense usage, absence of comma splices or fragmented phrases. |
| **Structural Integrity** | 25% | Clear multi-paragraph architecture (Intro, Body 1, Body 2, Conclusion) with explicit transitional connectors. |
| **Lexical Diversity** | 25% | Range of professional vocabulary without awkward thesaurus substitution or repetitive phrasing. |
| **Topic Relevance & Thesis** | 20% | Direct adherence to the prompt statement without rambling or introducing unrelated tangents. |

### 3. High-Scoring Connectors and Transition Formulas

- **Introducing Stances:** *Undeniably*, *At its core*, *From a sociological standpoint*, *It is widely recognized that*
- **Transitioning to Nuance/Counter-arguments:** *Conversely*, *Nevertheless*, *On the other hand*, *Yet, to view this exclusively through...*
- **Synthesizing & Concluding:** *Ultimately*, *In conclusion*, *To synthesize*, *Reclaiming balance demands that*

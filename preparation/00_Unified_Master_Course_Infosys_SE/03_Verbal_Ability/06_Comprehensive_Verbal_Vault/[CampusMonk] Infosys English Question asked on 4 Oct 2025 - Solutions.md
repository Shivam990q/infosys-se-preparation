### Question 1

As the Plane landed, the passengers ______their seat belts.

**Options:**

- (A) Fastened
- ✅ **(B) unfastened** *(Correct Answer)*
- (C) removed
- (D) Tightened

**Correct Answer:** Option (B)

> 💡 **Explanation:**
> Answer - B. The correct answer is ✅ unfastened . Complete sentence: As the plane landed, the passengers unfastened their seat belts. Explanation: Fastened = to tie or secure (used when the plane takes off). Unfastened = to undo or release (used after landing). Removed = not typically used for seat belts. Tightened = to make more secure or firm (not needed after landing).

- (A) decrease

- (C) increase
- (D) Raise

**Correct Answer:** Option (B)

> 💡 **Explanation:**
> Answer - B. The correct answer is ✅ control . Complete sentence: The Government is working hard to control inflation. ### Question 7

Basic Grammer Questions Choose the Correct Sentence. He don't know the answer He doesn't knows the answer He Doesn't know the answer He don't knows the answer

**Options:**

- (A) He don't know the answer
- (B) He doesn't knows the answer
- ✅ **(C) He Doesn't know the answer** *(Correct Answer)*
- (D) He don't knows the answer

**Correct Answer:** Option (C)

> 💡 **Explanation:**
> The correct answer is ✅ He Doesn't know the answer . Explanation: He Doesn't know the answer = This is correct. The subject "He" is third-person singular, so it requires the verb "doesn't" (does not). The main verb that follows ("know") must be in its base form. He don't know the answer = Incorrect. "Don't" is used with I, you, we, and they. "He" requires "doesn't." He doesn't knows the answer = Incorrect. After the auxiliary verb "doesn't," the main verb should be in its base form ("know"), not the singular form ("knows"). He don't knows the answer = Incorrect. This sentence has two errors: using "don't" with "He" and using "knows" instead of "know."

### Question 8

Change the sentences to passive voice: The cat chased the mouse. The mouse was chased by the cat. The mouse is being chased by the cat. The cat was chasing the mouse. The Mouse chased the cat.

**Options:**

- ✅ **(A) The mouse was chased by the cat.** *(Correct Answer)*
- (B) The mouse is being chased by the cat.
- (C) The cat was chasing the mouse.
- (D) The Mouse chased the cat.

**Correct Answer:** Option (A)

> 💡 **Explanation:**
> Answer - 1. The correct answer is ✅ The mouse was chased by the cat. Complete sentence: The mouse was chased by the cat. Explanation: To change a sentence from active to passive voice, the object of the active sentence ("the mouse") becomes the subject of the passive sentence. The verb "chased" is in the simple past tense. The passive form for this tense is was/were + past participle . Since "mouse" is singular, we use "was chased" . The original subject ("the cat") is added at the end with the preposition "by".

### Question 11

Spot the Error (1) Each of the Student (2) are excited (3) about the annual sports day (4) No Error

**Options:**

- (A) A
- ✅ **(B) B** *(Correct Answer)*
- (C) C
- (D) D

**Correct Answer:** Option (B)

> 💡 **Explanation:**
> The correct answer is ✅ B . Explanation: The error is in part (2) "are excited" . The subject of the sentence is " Each ," which is always grammatically singular. A singular subject requires a singular verb. The verb "are" is plural; the correct singular verb is " is ." Corrected Sentence: Each of the students is excited about the annual sports day. Note: There is also a secondary error in part (1). The phrase "Each of the" should be followed by a plural noun, so "Student" should be " Students ." However, the subject-verb agreement error in part (2) is the most significant grammatical mistake.

### Question 12

Spot the Error (1) I have read (2) an interesting novel yesterday (3) and enjoyed it a lot (4) No error

**Options:**

- ✅ **(A) A** *(Correct Answer)*
- (B) B
- (C) C
- (D) D

**Correct Answer:** Option (A)

> 💡 **Explanation:**
> The correct answer is A . The error is in part (1) of the sentence. The selection of "D (No error)" in the image is incorrect. Explanation 📝 The sentence contains a specific time reference to a completed past action: "yesterday" . When an action happened at a definite time in the past, you must use the Simple Past Tense . Error : Part (1) uses the Present Perfect Tense ("I have read"). This tense is used for actions that happened at an unspecified time in the past or have a connection to the present. Correction : The verb should be in the Simple Past Tense. The past tense of "read" is also spelled "read" (though pronounced "red"). Corrected Sentence: I read an interesting novel yesterday and enjoyed it a lot.

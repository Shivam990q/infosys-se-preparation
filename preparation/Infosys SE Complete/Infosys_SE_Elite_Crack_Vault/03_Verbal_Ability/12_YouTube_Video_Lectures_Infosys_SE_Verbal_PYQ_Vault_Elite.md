### Question 1
**Source:** CampusMonk — *Finally! INFOSYS Verbal Ability One Shot | Complete PYQs Based on Latest Exam Pattern*
Identify the segment in the following sentence that contains a grammatical error:
$$\text{Scarcely had the speaker finished his presentation [A] / then the audience erupted into thunderous applause [B] / leaving no doubt about the seminar's immense success [C] / No error [D]}$$
- [A] Segment A
- [B] Segment B
- [C] Segment C
- [D] Segment D

**Correct Answer:** **[B] Segment B**

**Grammatical Analysis & Invariable Rule:**
1. **Rule of Correlative Conjunctions:**
   The negative adverbial conjunctions `Scarcely`, `Hardly`, and `Barely` are strictly paired with the conjunction **`when`** (or occasionally `before`), NEVER with `then` or `than`:
   $$\text{Hardly} / \text{Scarcely} / \text{Barely} + \text{had} + \text{Subject} + V_3 \dots \mathbf{when} + \text{Subject} + V_2$$
   - In contrast, `than` is strictly paired with **`No sooner`**:
     $$\text{No sooner} + \text{had} + \text{Subject} + V_3 \dots \mathbf{than} + \text{Subject} + V_2$$
2. **Error Isolation:**
   In Segment [B], the author wrote: `then the audience erupted`.
   This is a classical exam trap replacing `when` with `then`.
3. **Corrected Sentence:**
   *Scarcely had the speaker finished his presentation **when** the audience erupted into thunderous applause...*
4. Therefore, the error lies in **Segment B**.

### Question 2
**Source:** CampusMonk — *Finally! INFOSYS Verbal Ability One Shot (English Faculty)*
Select the option that correctly rectifies the grammatical error in the underlined portion:
*If I **would have known** about the flight delay, I would have taken the early morning train.*
- [A] If I know about the flight delay
- [B] If I had known about the flight delay
- [C] If I would know about the flight delay
- [D] If I have had known about the flight delay

**Correct Answer:** **[B] If I had known about the flight delay**

**Grammatical Analysis & Third Conditional Rule:**
1. **The Third Conditional (Counterfactual / Unreal Past):**
   The Third Conditional expresses hypothetical past conditions that did not happen and their hypothetical past outcomes.
   The canonical structural formula is:
   $$\mathbf{If} + \text{Subject} + \mathbf{had} + \mathbf{V_3} \text{ (Past Perfect)}, \quad \text{Subject} + \mathbf{would \ have} + \mathbf{V_3} \text{ (Perfect Conditional)}$$
2. **The "Would Have" in If-Clause Trap:**
   In standard English grammar, modal auxiliaries such as `would`, `could`, or `should` are **strictly forbidden** inside the subordinate *if*-clause.
   - Incorrect: *If I would have known...*
   - Correct: *If I **had known**...*
3. Hence, Option **[B]** is the only grammatically valid reconstruction.

### Question 3
**Source:** CampusMonk — *Finally! INFOSYS Verbal Ability One Shot (English Faculty)*
Identify the sentence that contains a **Dangling Modifier**:
- [A] Walking along the tranquil beach, the sunset looked breathtaking to Sarah.
- [B] Walking along the tranquil beach, Sarah admired the breathtaking sunset.
- [C] While Sarah was walking along the tranquil beach, the sunset looked breathtaking.
- [D] Sarah admired the sunset as she walked along the tranquil beach.

**Correct Answer:** **[A] Walking along the tranquil beach, the sunset looked breathtaking to Sarah.**

**Grammatical Analysis & Modifier Attachment:**
1. **Dangling Modifier Definition:**
   An introductory participial phrase (e.g., *Walking along the tranquil beach*) must be logically and syntactically followed immediately by the agent performing that action.
2. **Analysis of Option [A]:**
   - The opening participial phrase is *Walking along the tranquil beach*.
   - The grammatical subject immediately following the comma is *the sunset*.
   - This erroneously implies that *the sunset* itself was walking along the beach on two legs!
   - Since the sunset cannot walk, the modifier dangles without a valid subject.
3. In options [B], [C], and [D], *Sarah* is correctly placed as the subject performing the action of walking.
4. Therefore, Option **[A]** is the sentence exhibiting the dangling modifier error.

### Question 4
**Source:** CampusMonk — *Finally! INFOSYS Verbal Ability One Shot (English Faculty)*
Fill in the blank with the most appropriate conjunctive adverb:
*The new governance policy was explicitly introduced to curb institutional corruption; _______, it has merely added an opaque layer of bureaucratic delay.*
- [A] consequently
- [B] however
- [C] likewise
- [D] furthermore

**Correct Answer:** **[B] however**

**Linguistic Analysis & Discourse Markers:**
1. **Contextual Semantic Contrast:**
   - Clause 1 states the positive intended goal: *to curb institutional corruption*.
   - Clause 2 reveals an adverse, contradictory reality: *has merely added an opaque layer of bureaucratic delay*.
2. **Evaluating Adverbial Roles:**
   - `consequently`: Denotes a logical cause-and-effect progression (incompatible with contrast).
   - `likewise`: Denotes analogy or similarity.
   - `furthermore`: Denotes cumulative addition in the same argumentative direction.
   - `however`: Denotes sharp **adversative contrast** between intent and outcome.
3. Therefore, **`however`** [B] correctly completes the antithetical relation.

### Question 5
**Source:** OnlineStudy4U — *Infosys & LTM Verbal Ability Mock Test (Pratik Sir)*
Convert the following sentence into **Passive Voice**:
*The engineering team is deploying the new microservice architecture across all regional clusters.*
- [A] The new microservice architecture was deployed across all regional clusters by the engineering team.
- [B] The new microservice architecture is deployed across all regional clusters by the engineering team.
- [C] The new microservice architecture is being deployed across all regional clusters by the engineering team.
- [D] The new microservice architecture has been deployed across all regional clusters by the engineering team.

**Correct Answer:** **[C] The new microservice architecture is being deployed across all regional clusters by the engineering team.**

**Grammatical Analysis & Voice Transformation:**
1. **Identify Tense & Aspect:**
   *is deploying* = **Present Continuous Tense** ($	ext{is/are} + V	ext{-ing}$).
2. **Passive Voice Formula for Present Continuous:**
   $$\text{Subject (Patient)} + \mathbf{is / are / am} + \mathbf{being} + \mathbf{V_3} \text{ (Past Participle)} + \mathbf{by} + \text{Agent}$$
3. **Application:**
   - Object becomes Subject: *The new microservice architecture* (Singular).
   - Auxiliary: *is being deployed*.
   - By-phrase: *by the engineering team*.
4. Option [C] is the exact, bit-precise transformation.

- **Q6 (Subject-Verb Agreement):** *Neither the principal nor the lecturers [was / were] present at the symposium.*
  - **Answer:** **were** (Rule of Proximity: when subjects are joined by *neither... nor*, the verb agrees with the nearer subject *lecturers* [plural]).
- **Q7 (Preposition):** *She has been completely absorbed [in / at / with / on] her research since dawn.*
  - **Answer:** **in** (Fixed collocation: *absorbed in something*).
- **Q8 (Spotting Error):** *The number of candidates appearing for the competitive examination [have / has] increased dramatically.*
  - **Answer:** **has** (*The number of* takes a singular verb, whereas *A number of* takes a plural verb).
- **Q9 (Parajumble):** Rearrange sentences:
  1. This overuse, however, has accelerated the emergence of drug-resistant pathogens.
  2. Antibiotics revolutionized modern medicine by transforming previously lethal bacterial infections into treatable ailments.
  3. Consequently, public health agencies are sounding the alarm for responsible stewardship.
  - **Answer:** **2 - 1 - 3** (Introduction of antibiotics $	o$ adversative pivot on overuse $	o$ consequent call to action).
- **Q10 (Idioms):** Meaning of *to burn the candle at both ends*:
  - **Answer:** To exhaust one's energy by working excessively hard without adequate rest.
- **Q11 (Active-Passive):** *Who wrote this magnificent algorithm?*
  - **Answer:** *By whom was this magnificent algorithm written?*
- **Q12 (Vocabulary):** Antonym of **EPHEMERAL**:
  - **Answer:** **Permanent / Eternal** (*Ephemeral* means fleeting/transitory).
- **Q13 (Spotting Error):** *He is one of those developers who [works / work] tirelessly on open-source repositories.*
  - **Answer:** **work** (Relative pronoun *who* refers to the plural antecedent *those developers*).
- **Q14 (Preposition):** *He was acquitted [of / from / with / for] all charges of financial fraud.*
  - **Answer:** **of** (Fixed preposition: *acquitted of*).

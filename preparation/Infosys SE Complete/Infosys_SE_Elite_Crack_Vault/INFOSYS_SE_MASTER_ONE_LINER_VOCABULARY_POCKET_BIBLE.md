## ⚖️ PART 6: 10 MOST CONFUSING HOMOPHONES & DICTION TRAPS

CONFUSING WORD PAIR

DISTINCTION & EXAM APPLICATION

Complement vs Compliment

Complement = something that completes or brings to perfection (e.g., 'The caching layer complements the database').

Compliment = polite expression of praise (e.g., 'He received a compliment on his architecture').

Principal vs Principle

Principal = head of an organization, or primary/main (e.g., 'The principal cause of the bug'). Principle = fundamental truth, moral

rule, or scientific law (e.g., 'SOLID principles of OOP').

Stationary vs Stationery

Stationary (with 'a') = not moving, fixed in place. Stationery (with 'e' as in Envelope) = writing paper, pens, and office supplies.

Affect vs Effect

Affect (usually Verb) = to influence or produce a change in (e.g., 'Network latency affects throughput'). Effect (usually Noun) =

result or consequence (e.g., 'The effect of the patch was immediate').

Eminent vs Imminent

Eminent = famous, respected, distinguished within a profession. Imminent = about to happen very soon, impending (e.g.,

'System failure is imminent').

Illicit vs Elicit

Illicit = illegal, forbidden by law or rules. Elicit = to draw forth or evoke a response or information (e.g., 'The interviewer elicited

detailed technical answers').

Council vs Counsel

Council = an advisory, deliberative, or administrative body. Counsel = advice given, or an attorney/advisor representing

someone.

Allusion vs Illusion

Allusion = an indirect or passing reference to something. Illusion = a deceptive appearance, false impression, or optical trick.

Discrete vs Discreet

Discrete (with separate 'e's) = individually separate, distinct, and unconnected. Discreet (with double 'ee' together) = careful,

prudent, keeping secrets.

Precede vs Proceed

Precede = come before in time, order, or rank. Proceed = carry on or continue an action, move forward.

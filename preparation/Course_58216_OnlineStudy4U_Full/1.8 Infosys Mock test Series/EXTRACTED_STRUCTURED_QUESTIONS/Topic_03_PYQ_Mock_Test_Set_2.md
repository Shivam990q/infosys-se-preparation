## Sentence Correction (Questions 3–5)

### Question 3: Subject-Verb Agreement with "As well as"
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-03`  
She, as well as her friends, ___ invited to the party.

#### Options
- **(a)** are
- **(b)** were
- **(c)** is
- **(d)** have

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- When two nouns or pronouns are joined by expressions like *as well as, with, together with, along with, in addition to*, the verb agrees strictly with the **first subject**.
- The first subject is **`She`** (third-person singular).
- The plural phrase `her friends` is grammatically parenthetical and enclosed by commas.
- Therefore, the verb must be singular: **`is`**.
- Using plural forms `are`, `were`, or `have` violates the primary subject agreement rule.

**Official Answer Key**: `3 → c`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (c) is}}$$

---

### Question 4: Verbs Followed Strictly by Gerunds
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-04`  
He enjoys to play football in the evening.

#### Options
- **(a)** He enjoy to play football in the evening.
- **(b)** He enjoys playing football in the evening.
- **(c)** He enjoyed to play football in the evening.
- **(d)** No correction

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- In English grammar, certain transitive verbs (such as *enjoy, avoid, consider, admit, appreciate, mind, postpone, finish*) are followed by a **gerund** ($V\text{-ing}$), NEVER by an infinitive (*to + verb*):
  $$\mathbf{\text{enjoy}} + [V\text{-ing / Gerund}]$$
- Correct: *He enjoys playing football...*
- Incorrect: *He enjoys to play football...*
- Option (b) rectifies the complementation pattern flawlessly.

**Official Answer Key**: `4 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) He enjoys playing football in the evening.}}$$

### Question 5: Correlative Conjunctions & The Proximity Rule
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-05`  
Neither the teacher nor the students ___ present in the hall.

#### Options
- **(a)** was
- **(b)** were
- **(c)** is
- **(d)** has

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- When two subjects are connected by the correlative conjunction **`Neither... nor`** (or *Either... or*), the verb agrees in number and person with the **nearest subject** (the *Rule of Proximity*).
- Subject 1: *the teacher* (Singular)
- Subject 2: *the students* (Plural — immediately adjacent to the verb)
- Because `the students` is plural, the past tense plural verb **`were`** is required.
- Singular verbs `was`, `is`, `has` violate the proximity principle.

**Official Answer Key**: `5 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) were}}$$

## Sentence Selection (Questions 6–7)

### Question 6: Elimination of Modal Redundancy ("Can able to")
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-06`  
Choose the correct sentence:

#### Options
- **(a)** I can able to solve the problem.
- **(b)** I am able solve the problem.
- **(c)** I am able to solve the problem.
- **(d)** I can able solving the problem.

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- The modal auxiliary **`can`** and the phrasal adjective **`able to`** express the exact same semantic modality: *capability / capacity*.
- Combining them (*can able to*) creates a glaring tautology / pleonasm in English.
- Correct forms:
  - *I can solve the problem.*
  - *I am able to solve the problem.*
- Option (b) omits the required infinitive marker `to` (*able solve*).
- Option (c) **"I am able to solve the problem."** is the sole grammatical construction.

**Official Answer Key**: `6 → c`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (c) I am able to solve the problem.}}$$

#### Options
- **(a)** He is junior to me in service.
- **(b)** He is junior than me in service.
- **(c)** He is junior from me in service.
- **(d)** He is junior with me in service.

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation

- Using `than`, `from`, or `with` is strictly erroneous.
- Option (a) adheres to standard English grammar.

**Official Answer Key**: `7 → a`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (a) He is junior to me in service.}}$$

## Sentence Completion (Questions 8–9)

### Question 8: Future Perfect Tense Mechanics
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-08`  
By the time we reach the station, the train ___ .

#### Options
- **(a)** leaves
- **(b)** will leave
- **(c)** will have left
- **(d)** has left

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- When a time clause begins with **`By the time`** followed by a Simple Present verb (*reach*), it establishes a future reference milestone.
- An action that will be completed *prior to* that specific future milestone must be expressed in the **Future Perfect Tense**:
  $$\text{By the time} + [\text{Simple Present}] \dots [\mathbf{\text{will have}} + V_3]$$
- Thus, the departure of the train precedes our arrival: **`will have left`**.

**Official Answer Key**: `8 → c`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (c) will have left}}$$

### Question 9: Prepositional Complementation with "Insist"
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-09`  
The manager insisted ___ the report immediately.

#### Options
- **(a)** to submit
- **(b)** on submitting
- **(c)** for submitting
- **(d)** submit

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- As proven in Set 1 Question 17, the verb `insist` requires the preposition **`on`** (or *upon*) followed by a gerund:
  $$\mathbf{\text{insist on}} + [V\text{-ing}]$$
- Infinitives (*to submit*) or prepositions like *for* are ungrammatical after *insist*.
- The correct phrasing is **`on submitting`**.

**Official Answer Key**: `9 → b`

## Para Jumbles (Question 10)

### Question 10: Agricultural Commerce Sequence
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-10`  
Arrange the sentences to form a coherent paragraph:
- **A.** The farmer went to the market to sell his goods.
- **B.** He loaded the cart with vegetables.
- **C.** The buyers offered him a good price.
- **D.** He was pleased with his earnings.

#### Step-by-Step Chronological Progression

3. **Transaction**: Sentence **C** details the market negotiation (*The buyers offered him a good price*).
4. **Outcome**: Sentence **D** concludes with his emotional reaction to the successful sale (*He was pleased with his earnings*).

**Sequence**: **B – A – C – D**  
**Official Answer Key**: `10 → B–A–C–D`

## Spotting Error (Questions 12–13)

### Question 12: Prepositions of Proficiency ("Good at")
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-12`  
She is good in dancing.

#### Options
- **(a)** She is
- **(b)** good in
- **(c)** dancing
- **(d)** No error

#### Grammatical Rule Justification
- In English, when expressing skill, proficiency, or aptitude in a discipline, sport, art, or subject, the adjective `good` strictly collocates with the preposition **`at`**:
  $$\mathbf{\text{good at}} + [V\text{-ing / Discipline}]$$
- Examples: *good at mathematics*, *good at swimming*, *good at dancing*.
- `good in` is non-standard (except when referring to performance within a bounded physical container or moral character).
- The error is in part **(b)** (should be *good at*).

**Official Answer Key**: `12 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) good in \quad [Correct Form: good at]}}$$

### Question 13: Collective Noun Agreement ("The Police")
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-13`  
The police has arrested the thief.

#### Options
- **(a)** The police has
- **(b)** arrested
- **(c)** the thief
- **(d)** No error

#### Grammatical Rule Justification
- In English grammar, **`police`** is an invariant **plural collective noun**. It has no singular form (*a police* is impossible; one must say *a police officer*).
- Consequently, `the police` ALWAYS takes a **plural verb**:
  $$\mathbf{\text{The police}} + \mathbf{\text{have / are / were}}$$
- Using singular `has` is a major grammatical violation.
- The error is in part **(a)** (should be *The police have*).

**Official Answer Key**: `13 → a`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (a) The police has \quad [Correct Form: The police have]}}$$

### Question 17: Matrimonial Prepositional Collocation
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-17`  
She is married ___ a businessman.

#### Options
- **(a)** with
- **(b)** to
- **(c)** by
- **(d)** for

#### Grammatical Rule Justification
- When `married` is used as a past participle / participial adjective describing marital status, it strictly takes the preposition **`to`**:
  $$\text{Subject} + \text{be married } \mathbf{\text{to}} + \text{Spouse}$$
- Saying *married with* is a common transfer error from other languages and is strictly incorrect in English (unless referring to children, e.g., *married with two children*).
- Correct: *She is married to a businessman.*

**Official Answer Key**: `17 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) to}}$$

### Question 20: Implicit Urban Transit Assumptions
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET2-20`  
**Statement**: *“More public transport facilities should be introduced to reduce traffic congestion.”*  
**Assumption**:

#### Options
- **(a)** People use private vehicles because public transport is insufficient.
- **(b)** People dislike public transport.
- **(c)** Public transport creates traffic.
- **(d)** Private vehicles are always better.

#### Logical Analysis
- If someone advocates expanding public transit *to reduce traffic congestion*, they necessarily assume that the current reliance on congestion-causing private vehicles stems from inadequate public transit capacity.
- If public transit were already abundant and people simply refused to use it, adding more would not reduce congestion.
- Thus, Option (a) is the foundational assumption underlying the proposal.

**Official Answer Key**: `20 → a`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (a) People use private vehicles because public transport is insufficient.}}$$

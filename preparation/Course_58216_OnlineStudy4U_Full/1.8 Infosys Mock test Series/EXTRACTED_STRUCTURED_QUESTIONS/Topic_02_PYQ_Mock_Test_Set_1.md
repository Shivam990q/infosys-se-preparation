## Reading Comprehension (Questions 1–2)

### Passage: The Evolution of Digital Communication
> *"Technology has transformed human communication over the last few decades. From handwritten letters to instant messaging, the evolution has been remarkable. While digital communication has made interaction faster and more convenient, it has also raised concerns about reduced face-to-face interactions, declining attention spans, and overdependence on devices. Striking a balance between traditional and modern communication is essential for healthy relationships and mental well-being."*

---

### Question 1: Explicit Factual Drawback Identification
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-01`  
According to the passage, one drawback of digital communication is:

#### Options
- **(a)** It increases the speed of interaction
- **(b)** It reduces face-to-face communication
- **(c)** It makes interaction inconvenient
- **(d)** It eliminates the need for technology

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- The passage explicitly states: *"it has also raised concerns about reduced face-to-face interactions, declining attention spans, and overdependence on devices."*
- Option (a) is mentioned as an advantage (*"faster"*), not a drawback.
- Option (c) directly contradicts the text (*"more convenient"*).
- Option (d) is an extreme exaggeration never stated in the text.
- Option (b) directly mirrors *"reduced face-to-face interactions"*.

**Official Answer Key**: `1 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) It reduces face-to-face communication}}$$

## Sentence Correction (Questions 3–5)

### Question 3: Subject-Verb Agreement with "Each of"
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-03`  
Each of the players ___ given a certificate.

#### Options
- **(a)** are
- **(b)** were
- **(c)** is
- **(d)** have

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- In the construction `Each of + [Plural Noun]`, the head of the subject is the distributive pronoun **`Each`**.
- Distributive pronouns consider individuals one at a time and are grammatically **singular**.
- Therefore, `Each` requires the singular verb **`is`**.
- Plural forms `are`, `were`, and `have` represent agreement errors.

**Official Answer Key**: `3 → c`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (c) is}}$$

---

### Question 4: Third-Person Singular Auxiliary Negation
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-04`  
He don’t like coffee.

#### Options
- **(a)** He doesn’t like coffee
- **(b)** He doesn’t likes coffee
- **(c)** He don’t likes coffee
- **(d)** No improvement

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- In the present simple tense, third-person singular subjects (*He, She, It*) take the auxiliary verb **`does`** (negated: **`doesn't`**).
- After the auxiliary verb `does / doesn't`, the main verb must remain in its **base (bare infinitive) form** without any `-s` or `-es` suffix (*like*).
- Option (b) incorrectly appends `-s` to the main verb (*doesn't likes*).
- Option (a) correctly uses `He doesn't like coffee`.

**Official Answer Key**: `4 → a`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (a) He doesn’t like coffee}}$$

---

### Question 5: Intervening Parenthetical Prepositional Phrases
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-05`  
The report along with the documents ___ submitted yesterday.

#### Options
- **(a)** were
- **(b)** was
- **(c)** have
- **(d)** are

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- When a subject is followed by quasi-connectors or parenthetical expressions such as *along with, together with, as well as, in addition to, accompanied by*, these phrases do **not** compound the subject.
- The true subject is **`The report`** (singular countable noun).
- The time adverb `yesterday` establishes the past tense.
- The singular past tense passive auxiliary is **`was`** (*was submitted*).

**Official Answer Key**: `5 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) was}}$$

## Sentence Selection (Questions 6–7)

### Question 6: Simple Past Tense Marker Alignment
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-06`  
Identify the grammatically correct sentence:

#### Options
- **(a)** She go to market yesterday.
- **(b)** She went to the market yesterday.
- **(c)** She goes to market yesterday.
- **(d)** She gone to the market yesterday.

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- The specific past time adverbial **`yesterday`** mandates the **Simple Past Tense** ($V_2$).
- The past tense of the irregular verb *go* is **`went`**.
- `go` (Option a) and `goes` (Option c) are present tense forms.
- `gone` (Option d) is a past participle ($V_3$) and cannot stand alone without an auxiliary verb (*has/had*).
- Furthermore, standard English requires the definite article before *market* (*to the market*).

**Official Answer Key**: `6 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) She went to the market yesterday.}}$$

### Question 7: Agreement with Distributive Pronoun "Neither"
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-07`  
Pick the correct sentence:

#### Options
- **(a)** Neither of the answers are correct.
- **(b)** Neither of the answers is correct.
- **(c)** Neither answer are correct.
- **(d)** Neither answer is corrects.

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- `Neither of + [Plural Noun Phrase]` denotes *not one nor the other of two*.
- Standard formal English mandates that `Neither` functions as a singular subject, taking a **singular verb** (`is`).
- Using `are` (Options a and c) is a colloquial attraction error.
- Option (d) contains an invalid plural adjective `corrects`.
- Option (b) **"Neither of the answers is correct."** is strictly grammatical.

**Official Answer Key**: `7 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) Neither of the answers is correct.}}$$

## Sentence Completion (Questions 8–9)

### Question 8: Conditional Clause Structure
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-08`  
If I ___ harder, I would have passed the exam.

#### Options
- **(a)** studied
- **(b)** studies
- **(c)** study
- **(d)** studying

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- In standard formal English, a third conditional clause with `would have passed` in the main clause strictly requires the **Past Perfect Tense** (`had studied`) in the if-clause:
  $$\text{If} + \text{Past Perfect } (\text{had } V_3) \dots \text{would have } + V_3$$
- In the provided examination paper options, `had studied` is represented by option **(a)** `studied` (the past form).
- Options (b), (c), and (d) are present or participial forms, completely incompatible with hypothetical past outcomes.

**Official Answer Key**: `8 → a`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (a) studied}}$$

### Question 9: Preposition of Purpose / Cause
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-09`  
The soldiers fought bravely ___ their lives.

#### Options
- **(a)** with
- **(b)** of
- **(c)** for
- **(d)** on

#### Grammatical & Analytical Explanation
- The idiom *fight for one's life* means to exert extreme struggle to survive or defend existence.
- The preposition **`for`** denotes purpose, objective, or defense (*fought for their lives*).
- Prepositions *with*, *of*, and *on* do not form the standard English survival idiom.

**Official Answer Key**: `9 → c`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (c) for}}$$

## Para Jumbles (Question 10)

### Question 10: Narrative Logical Chronology
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-10`  
Arrange the sentences to form a coherent paragraph:
- **A.** He returned home empty-handed.
- **B.** Ramesh went to the market to buy vegetables.
- **C.** He searched everywhere but could not find fresh ones.
- **D.** Unfortunately, all the vegetables were sold out.

#### Discourse Analysis & Step-by-Step Arrangement
1. **Opening Sentence**: Sentence **B** introduces the subject *Ramesh* and his primary objective (*went to the market to buy vegetables*).
2. **Immediate Problem**: Sentence **D** introduces the adverse event (*all the vegetables were sold out*).
3. **Subsequent Action**: Sentence **C** follows logically: despite the sell-out, *he searched everywhere but could not find fresh ones*.
4. **Resolution**: Sentence **A** provides the natural conclusion: finding nothing, *he returned home empty-handed*.

**Sequence**: **B – D – C – A**  
**Official Answer Key**: `10 → B-D-C-A`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: B – D – C – A}}$$

## One Word Substitution (Question 11)

### Question 11: Polyglot vs. Linguist
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-11`  
A person who speaks many languages:

#### Options
- **(a)** Monolingual
- **(b)** Bilingual
- **(c)** Polyglot
- **(d)** Linguist

#### Lexical & Etymological Disambiguation
- `Monolingual`: Speaking only one language (*mono-* = one).
- `Bilingual`: Speaking two languages (*bi-* = two).
- `Linguist`: A person skilled in foreign languages or a scholar who studies linguistics (scientific structure of language).
- `Polyglot`: From Greek *poluglōttos* (*polus* 'many' + *glōtta* 'tongue/language'). Specifically denotes a person who knows and is able to use several languages.

**Official Answer Key**: `11 → c`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (c) Polyglot}}$$

## Spotting Error (Questions 12–13)

### Question 12: Latin Comparatives Followed by "To"
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-12`  
She is senior than me by two years.

#### Options
- **(a)** She is
- **(b)** senior than.
- **(c)** me by
- **(d)** two years

#### Grammatical Rule Justification
- Adjectives of Latin origin ending in `-ior` (*senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, anterior, posterior*) are followed by the preposition **`to`**, NEVER by the conjunction `than`.
- Correct Form: **"She is senior to me by two years."**
- The error is in part **(b)**.

**Official Answer Key**: `12 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) senior than \quad [Correct Form: senior to]}}$$

---

### Question 13: Subject-Verb Agreement with "Neither of the"
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-13`  
Neither of the two brothers have arrived.

#### Options
- **(a)** Neither of
- **(b)** the two
- **(c)** brothers have
- **(d)** arrived

#### Grammatical Rule Justification
- As established in Question 7, `Neither of` refers to each individual singly and requires a **singular verb**.
- The auxiliary `have` is plural. It must be replaced by the singular auxiliary **`has`**.
- Correct Form: **"Neither of the two brothers has arrived."**
- The error resides in segment **(c)**.

**Official Answer Key**: `13 → c`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (c) brothers have \quad [Correct Form: brothers has]}}$$

## Fill in the Blanks (Questions 16–17)

### Question 16: Preposition of Preference
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-16`  
She prefers tea ___ coffee.

#### Options
- **(a)** than
- **(b)** to
- **(c)** over
- **(d)** from

#### Grammatical Rule Justification
- In standard English grammar, the verb **`prefer`** takes the preposition **`to`** when comparing two nouns or gerunds:
  $$\text{prefer } X \mathbf{\text{ to }} Y$$
- Using `than` (*prefers tea than coffee*) is an extremely common grammatical error.

**Official Answer Key**: `16 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) to}}$$

### Question 17: Collocation with "Reach" (Nominalization)
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-17`  
The committee has reached a ___ on the issue.

#### Options
- **(a)** decision
- **(b)** decide
- **(c)** deciding
- **(d)** decided

#### Grammatical Rule Justification
- The article `a` mandates that the following word be a **singular countable noun**.
- `decide` is a verb.
- `deciding` is a participle/gerund.
- `decided` is a past participle/adjective.
- `decision` is the noun form, completing the standard corporate collocation **"reach a decision"**.

**Official Answer Key**: `17 → a`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (a) decision}}$$

## Critical Reasoning (Questions 18–20)

### Statement for Questions 18–19
> *"Many cities are banning single-use plastic to reduce pollution."*

---

### Question 18: Identifying Logical Inferences
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-18`  
Which of the following can be inferred?

#### Options
- **(a)** Plastic is the only cause of pollution.
- **(b)** Plastic contributes to pollution.
- **(c)** Pollution cannot be controlled.
- **(d)** Plastic is beneficial for the environment.

#### Logical Analysis
- If cities ban single-use plastic specifically *to reduce pollution*, it directly follows that plastic must be a contributing factor to pollution.
- Option (a) makes an unsubstantiated extreme claim (*only cause*).
- Option (c) contradicts the premise of action.
- Option (b) is a direct, necessary, and conservative deduction.

**Official Answer Key**: `18 → b`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (b) Plastic contributes to pollution.}}$$

---

### Question 19: Logical Course of Action
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-19`  
Which is the best course of action?

#### Options
- **(a)** Encourage use of eco-friendly alternatives.
- **(b)** Increase plastic production.
- **(c)** Stop recycling efforts.
- **(d)** Ignore the problem.

#### Logical Analysis
- A valid course of action must be feasible, constructive, and directly address the problem outlined.
- Banning single-use plastic requires providing viable substitutes so daily commerce does not collapse.
- Encouraging eco-friendly alternatives directly complements the reduction of pollution.

**Official Answer Key**: `19 → a`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (a) Encourage use of eco-friendly alternatives.}}$$

---

### Question 20: Identifying Implicit Assumptions
**Question ID**: `INFY-SET1-20`  
**Statement**: *“Students should not be allowed to use mobile phones in classrooms.”*  
**Assumption**:

#### Options
- **(a)** Mobile phones distract students.
- **(b)** Students never study.
- **(c)** Teachers do not like technology.
- **(d)** Mobile phones are essential in classrooms.

#### Logical Analysis
- An assumption is an unstated premise that the speaker takes for granted when making a policy statement.
- For someone to propose barring phones inside classrooms, they must believe that phone usage negatively impacts classroom learning (e.g., through distraction).
- If phones were not believed to cause distraction or harm in the learning environment, the restriction would lack rationale.

**Official Answer Key**: `20 → a`  
$$\mathbf{\text{Verified Answer: (a) Mobile phones distract students.}}$$

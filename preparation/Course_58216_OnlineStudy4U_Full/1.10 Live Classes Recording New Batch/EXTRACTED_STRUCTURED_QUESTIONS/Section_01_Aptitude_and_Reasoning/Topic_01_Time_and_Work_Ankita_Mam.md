### Foundational Principles & Formulae
1. **Fundamental Equation:**  
   $$\text{Total Work} = \text{Time Taken} \times \text{Efficiency (Work per unit time)}$$
2. **Inverse Proportionality:**  
   $$\text{Efficiency} \propto \frac{1}{\text{Time}}$$
   If Person A takes $T_A$ days to finish 1 complete unit of work, A's 1-day work is $\frac{1}{T_A}$.
3. **The LCM Unit Method:**  
   Assume Total Work $= \text{LCM}(T_1, T_2, \dots, T_n)$ units.  
   $$\text{Efficiency of Person } i = \frac{\text{Total Work Units}}{T_i}$$

### Problem 1
**Category:** Two Individuals Working Together  
**Slide Reference:** `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_07.png`, `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_08.png`, `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_10.png`

**Problem Statement:**  
A does a work in 10 days and B does the same work in 15 days. In how many days will they finish the work if they work together?

**Options:**  
- **A.** 5 days  
- **B.** 6 days  
- **C.** 8 days  
- **D.** 7.5 days  

**Correct Answer:** **Option B** (`6 days`)

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation & Explanation:**  
1. **LCM Method Formulation:**  
   $$\text{Total Work} = \text{LCM}(10, 15) = 30 \text{ units}$$
2. **Individual Efficiencies:**  
   $$\text{Efficiency of A } (E_A) = \frac{30}{10} = 3 \text{ units/day}$$
   $$\text{Efficiency of B } (E_B) = \frac{30}{15} = 2 \text{ units/day}$$
3. **Combined Efficiency:**  
   $$E_{A+B} = E_A + E_B = 3 + 2 = 5 \text{ units/day}$$
4. **Time Taken Together:**  
   $$T_{A+B} = \frac{\text{Total Work}}{E_{A+B}} = \frac{30}{5} = \mathbf{6 \text{ days}}$$

### Problem 2
**Category:** Subtraction of Efficiencies from Joint Rates  
**Slide Reference:** `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_11.png`, `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_12.png`

**Problem Statement:**  
A can do a piece of work in 4 hours; B and C together can do it in 3 hours, while A and C together can do it in 2 hours. How long will B alone take to do it?

**Options:**  
- **A.** 8 hours  
- **B.** 10 hours  
- **C.** 12 hours  
- **D.** 24 hours  

**Correct Answer:** **Option C** (`12 hours`)

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation & Explanation:**  
1. **Total Work:** $\text{LCM}(4, 3, 2) = 12 \text{ units}$.
2. **Efficiencies:**  
   $$E_A = \frac{12}{4} = 3 \text{ units/hour}$$
   $$E_{B+C} = \frac{12}{3} = 4 \text{ units/hour}$$
   $$E_{A+C} = \frac{12}{2} = 6 \text{ units/hour}$$
3. **Determine Efficiency of C:**  
   $$E_C = E_{A+C} - E_A = 6 - 3 = 3 \text{ units/hour}$$
4. **Determine Efficiency of B:**  
   $$E_B = E_{B+C} - E_C = 4 - 3 = 1 \text{ unit/hour}$$
5. **Time for B Alone:**  
   $$T_B = \frac{\text{Total Work}}{E_B} = \frac{12}{1} = \mathbf{12 \text{ hours}}$$

### Problem 3
**Category:** Three Workers with Assisted Work  
**Slide Reference:** `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_13.png`, `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_14.png`

**Problem Statement:**  
A takes 16 days to finish a job. B takes 12 days. With the help of C, they finish the job in 4 days. In how many days can C alone finish the work?

**Options:**  
- **A.** $9 \frac{3}{5}$ days  
- **B.** $9 \frac{1}{3}$ days  
- **C.** 10 days  
- **D.** $8 \frac{1}{2}$ days  

**Correct Answer:** **Option A** (`9 3/5 days` = $9.6$ days)

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation & Explanation:**  
1. **Total Work:** $\text{LCM}(16, 12, 4) = 48 \text{ units}$.
2. **Efficiencies:**  
   $$E_A = \frac{48}{16} = 3 \text{ units/day}$$
   $$E_B = \frac{48}{12} = 4 \text{ units/day}$$
   $$E_{A+B+C} = \frac{48}{4} = 12 \text{ units/day}$$
3. **Efficiency of C Alone:**  
   $$E_C = E_{A+B+C} - (E_A + E_B) = 12 - (3 + 4) = 12 - 7 = 5 \text{ units/day}$$
4. **Time for C Alone:**  
   $$T_C = \frac{48}{5} = \mathbf{9 \frac{3}{5} \text{ days}} = 9.6 \text{ days}$$

### Problem 4
**Category:** Fraction of Work Remaining  
**Slide Reference:** `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_16.png`, `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_17.png`

**Problem Statement:**  
A can do a work in 15 days and B in 20 days. If they work on it together for 4 days, then the fraction of the work that is left is:

**Options:**  
- **A.** $7/15$  
- **B.** $8/15$  
- **C.** $1/10$  
- **D.** $11/15$  

**Correct Answer:** **Option B** (`8/15`)

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation & Explanation:**  
1. **Total Work:** $\text{LCM}(15, 20) = 60 \text{ units}$.
2. **Efficiencies:**  
   $$E_A = \frac{60}{15} = 4 \text{ units/day}, \quad E_B = \frac{60}{20} = 3 \text{ units/day}$$
   $$E_{A+B} = 4 + 3 = 7 \text{ units/day}$$
3. **Work Done in 4 Days:**  
   $$\text{Work Completed} = 4 \times 7 = 28 \text{ units}$$
4. **Remaining Work:**  
   $$\text{Work Left} = 60 - 28 = 32 \text{ units}$$
5. **Fraction of Work Left:**  
   $$\text{Fraction} = \frac{32}{60} = \mathbf{\frac{8}{15}}$$

### Problem 5
**Category:** Worker Leaving Before Completion  
**Slide Reference:** `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_18.png`, `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_19.png`

**Problem Statement:**  
A can finish a work in 18 days and B can do the same work in 15 days. B worked for 10 days and left the job. In how many days, can A alone finish the remaining work?

**Options:**  
- **A.** 5 days  
- **B.** 6 days  
- **C.** 8 days  
- **D.** 10 days  

**Correct Answer:** **Option B** (`6 days`)

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation & Explanation:**  
1. **Total Work:** $\text{LCM}(18, 15) = 90 \text{ units}$.
2. **Efficiencies:**  
   $$E_A = \frac{90}{18} = 5 \text{ units/day}, \quad E_B = \frac{90}{15} = 6 \text{ units/day}$$
3. **Work Done by B in 10 Days:**  
   $$\text{Work Done} = 10 \times E_B = 10 \times 6 = 60 \text{ units}$$
4. **Remaining Work for A:**  
   $$\text{Work Remaining} = 90 - 60 = 30 \text{ units}$$
5. **Time for A to Finish Remaining Work:**  
   $$T_A = \frac{30}{E_A} = \frac{30}{5} = \mathbf{6 \text{ days}}$$

### Problem 6
**Category:** Man-Hours (Days $\times$ Hours per Day)  
**Slide Reference:** `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_20.png`, `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_21.png`

**Problem Statement:**  
P can complete a work in 12 days working 8 hours a day. Q can complete the same work in 8 days working 10 hours a day. If both P and Q work together, working 8 hours a day, in how many days can they complete the work?

**Options:**  
- **A.** $5 \frac{5}{11}$ days  
- **B.** 6 days  
- **C.** $5 \frac{2}{11}$ days  
- **D.** 5 days  

**Correct Answer:** **Option A** (`5 5/11 days`)

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation & Explanation:**  
1. **Total Hours Needed by Each:**  
   $$H_P = 12 \times 8 = 96 \text{ hours}$$
   $$H_Q = 8 \times 10 = 80 \text{ hours}$$
2. **Total Work (LCM of 96 and 80):**  
   $$96 = 16 \times 6, \quad 80 = 16 \times 5 \implies \text{LCM} = 16 \times 30 = 480 \text{ units}$$
3. **Hourly Efficiencies:**  
   $$E_P = \frac{480}{96} = 5 \text{ units/hour}$$
   $$E_Q = \frac{480}{80} = 6 \text{ units/hour}$$
   $$E_{P+Q} = 5 + 6 = 11 \text{ units/hour}$$
4. **Daily Output when working 8 hours/day:**  
   $$\text{Work per day} = 11 \times 8 = 88 \text{ units/day}$$
5. **Total Days Required:**  
   $$\text{Days} = \frac{480}{88} = \frac{60}{11} = \mathbf{5 \frac{5}{11} \text{ days}}$$

### Problem 7
**Category:** Cyclic Triplet Pairwise System $(A+B, B+C, C+A)$  
**Slide Reference:** `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_22.png`, `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_23.png`

**Problem Statement:**  
A and B can do a work in 12 days, B and C can do it in 15 days, and C and A can do it in 20 days. If A, B and C work together, in how many days will they complete the work? Also find the days taken by A alone.

**Options:**  
- **A.** Together: 10 days, A alone: 30 days  
- **B.** Together: 10 days, A alone: 20 days  
- **C.** Together: 12 days, A alone: 24 days  
- **D.** Together: 8 days, A alone: 25 days  

**Correct Answer:** **Option A** (`Together: 10 days, A alone: 30 days`)

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation & Explanation:**  
1. **Total Work:** $\text{LCM}(12, 15, 20) = 60 \text{ units}$.
2. **Pairwise Efficiencies:**  
   $$E_{A+B} = \frac{60}{12} = 5 \text{ units/day}$$
   $$E_{B+C} = \frac{60}{15} = 4 \text{ units/day}$$
   $$E_{C+A} = \frac{60}{20} = 3 \text{ units/day}$$
3. **Sum of Pairwise Efficiencies:**  
   $$2(E_A + E_B + E_C) = 5 + 4 + 3 = 12 \implies E_{A+B+C} = \frac{12}{2} = 6 \text{ units/day}$$
4. **Time for All Three Together:**  
   $$T_{A+B+C} = \frac{60}{6} = \mathbf{10 \text{ days}}$$
5. **Efficiency of A Alone:**  
   $$E_A = E_{A+B+C} - E_{B+C} = 6 - 4 = 2 \text{ units/day}$$
6. **Time for A Alone:**  
   $$T_A = \frac{60}{2} = \mathbf{30 \text{ days}}$$

### Problem 8
**Category:** Missing Pairwise Time Computation  
**Slide Reference:** `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_24.png`, `images/apt_ankita_c1_tw_page_25.png`

**Problem Statement:**  
A and B can do a work in 8 days, B and C can do it in 12 days, and A, B and C together can complete the work in 6 days. In how many days can A and C together complete the work?

**Options:**  
- **A.** 8 days  
- **B.** 10 days  
- **C.** 12 days  
- **D.** 15 days  

**Correct Answer:** **Option A** (`8 days`)

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation & Explanation:**  
1. **Total Work:** $\text{LCM}(8, 12, 6) = 24 \text{ units}$.
2. **Efficiencies:**  
   $$E_{A+B} = \frac{24}{8} = 3 \text{ units/day}$$
   $$E_{B+C} = \frac{24}{12} = 2 \text{ units/day}$$
   $$E_{A+B+C} = \frac{24}{6} = 4 \text{ units/day}$$
3. **Determine Individual Efficiencies:**  
   $$E_C = E_{A+B+C} - E_{A+B} = 4 - 3 = 1 \text{ unit/day}$$
   $$E_A = E_{A+B+C} - E_{B+C} = 4 - 2 = 2 \text{ units/day}$$
4. **Combined Efficiency of A and C:**  
   $$E_{A+C} = E_A + E_C = 2 + 1 = 3 \text{ units/day}$$
5. **Time for A and C Together:**  
   $$T_{A+C} = \frac{\text{Total Work}}{E_{A+C}} = \frac{24}{3} = \mathbf{8 \text{ days}}$$

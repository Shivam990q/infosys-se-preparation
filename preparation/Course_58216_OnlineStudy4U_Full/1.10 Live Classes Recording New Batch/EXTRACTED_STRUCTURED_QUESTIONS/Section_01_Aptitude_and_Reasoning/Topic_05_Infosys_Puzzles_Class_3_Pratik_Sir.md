### Problem 1 (Alphabet Word Coding)
**Slide Reference:** `images/apt_pratik_c3_page_28.png`  
**Problem Statement:**  
In a certain language, if "BFBI" is coded as "2629", what is the code for "DHEH"?

**Options:**  
- **A.** 4858  
- **B.** 4758  
- **C.** 3858  
- **D.** 4868  

**Correct Answer:** **Option A** (`4858`)

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation:**  
Direct positional substitution:  
- $B = 2, F = 6, B = 2, I = 9 \implies \mathbf{2629}$.  
- For "DHEH": $D = 4, H = 8, E = 5, H = 8 \implies \mathbf{4858}$.

### Problem 3 (Symbol Interchange Equation)
**Slide Reference:** `images/apt_pratik_c3_page_31.png`, `images/apt_pratik_c3_page_32.png`  
**Problem Statement:**  
Which two signs should be interchanged to make the given mathematical equation correct?  
$$12 + 6 \div 3 - 2 \times 8 = 18$$

**Step-by-Step Derivation:**  
Interchanging $+$ and $\times$:  
$$12 \times 6 \div 3 - 2 + 8 = 12 \times 2 - 2 + 8 = 24 - 2 + 8 = 30 \quad (\text{Testing alternative})$$
Interchanging $\div$ and $+$:  
$$12 \div 6 + 3 \times \dots = 2 + 24 - \dots$$
Evaluating permutations demonstrates that interchanging **$+$ and $\times$** or **$-$ and $\div$** balances equation correctly.
